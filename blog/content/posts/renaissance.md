## About
**[The 400 Blows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_400_Blows)** ( French: _Les quatre cents coups_) is a 1959 French coming of age drama film clocking a total of 96 minutes. This movie was the debut of [François Truffaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Truffaut) as a director, (fun fact - his first non-french movie was the adaption of the classic science fiction novel [_Fahrenheit 451_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit_451_(1966_film)), and was his only English-speaking film, filmed in England, was a great challenge for Truffaut, because he barely spoke English himself & this was also Truffaut's first film in colour.) 
The 400 Blows is a film about Antoine Doinel, an adolescent(probably) in Paris who is often misunderstood, and  struggles with his parents and teachers due to his rebellious behavior, defining the French New Wave movement, and has four sequels: _Antoine et Colette_, _Stolen Kisses_, _Bed and Board_, and *Love on the Run*. 

I watched it on [Mubi](https://mubi.com/en/in/films/the-400-blows) but I think it's also available on Prime Video. I recommend watching the film as the following is my review of how I perceived the film, I'll be mostly talking about cinematography of the film as I found it really good among the standards that were set during that time.

## Plot
The movie is set in Paris and seeing the French culture in the 1950s was quite mesmerising experience for me. People walking down in their coats and beautiful attire really helps to capture the liveliness of Paris during the 1950s for the viewer’s through its detailed and elaborate visualization of Paris’ architecture, buildings, bustling streets, etc.

Antoine Doinel lives with his mother and stepfather in a tiny little apartment, they are poor and his parents are generally inattentive. The school makes his life more miserable and unbearable than at home thanks to his ruthless teacher who is a total nightmare, grabbing students by neck, throwing them out of class, shouting, taunting them. His only escape from the shackles of everyday life is to bunk off school with his one friend, going to cinema, smoking cigars, exploring the city of Paris.

## Cinematography
One thing that strike out to me was the cinematography of this film as in the camera movements felt ~~odd~~ and circular as if I am looking at the film through the eyes of a fish when the camera moves, at certain moments, after a bit of research(wikipedia :) ) I came to know that it shot in the [anamorphic format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anamorphic_format "Anamorphic format") [DyaliScope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_anamorphic_format_trade_names "List of anamorphic format trade names") .

The movie starts on a travelling shot down in the streets of Paris, carefully blending The Eiffel Tower in the distance ensuring that my attention is naturally drawn to it throughout the opening sequence. The camera moves and cuts to a new tracking shot bringing the Eiffel Tower slightly closer each time. Keeping it the centre of attention the camera runs parallel. in circular motion around it(trust me bro, just see it, it's beautiful). The most beautiful thing about this shot is that it can be seen as the POV of a child riding a car around Paris whose attention is always fixated on the Tower which is just there, just like we used to see the moon in night while travelling when we used to be small.

There is one notable instance throughout the film which uses the newfound technique of the long take; a shot that films a scene for a long period of time .
The film uses the 'long take technique' in the film which was newly found around that time which is a shot that is films a scene for a long period of time usually accompanied with the camera panning from a higher location (not necessarily though). This technique can be observed in one scene, as the teacher and the boys run through the streets of Paris, the camera pans from a higher, wider view, and captures the Paris cityscape of the time as the boys sneakily run away from the group into buildings and alleyways. This allows the film to direct the viewer’s attentions towards significant narrative elements. In this case, this technique really helps to capture the liveliness of Paris during the 1950s for the viewer’s through its detailed and elaborate visualization of Paris’  buildings, bustling streets, etc. Ultimately capturing the liveliness of the city.

I really like that the camera movements are motivated by actions for example the camera acts as a quick pan to the teacher in a sense acting as the sudden movement of the eyes. If the previous MCU was us observing the drawing, objectively - then the pan signifies also a subjective shot as if to yank our attention and place it at the teacher. The camera pansa as it follows also a subjective shot as if to yank out attetion and place it at the teacher. The camera pans as it follows our main character, and it even pans back at the teacher after Antoine shakes his fist at the guys who got him caught. Think for a moment why that pan happened? If you remember your school days and you were doing something like passing notes - you never wanted to get caught and you'd always look at the teacher, to see if he's looking. The camera pan is doing essentially that - acting as your eyes. So after the shaken fist , you'd look at the teacher to see if he noticed it because you wouldn't want to get caught.
